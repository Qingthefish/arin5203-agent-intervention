\section{Motivation and Research Question}

Tool-using agents are increasingly asked to operate AI infrastructure rather
than only answer questions. A platform agent may adjust a GPU quota, preempt a
workload, transfer resources between tenants, roll out a model, or reclaim
capacity. These actions are useful because they change external state, but an
incorrect call can interrupt production, cross an authorization boundary, or
remove a recovery path. Safety also depends on context: the same tool may be
routine in a sandbox with scoped approval and unacceptable during an incident
or across tenants.

This project asks one falsifiable question: \emph{before a proposed platform
mutation, can a calibrated gate reduce unsafe autonomous execution while
preserving useful coverage by choosing among Execute, Confirm, and Handoff?}
Here Confirm means that one additional round of valid user or owner evidence
can make the action safe. Handoff is reserved for residual operational risk or
policy conflict that ordinary confirmation cannot repair. A deterministic
first stage may also Block malformed or strictly inadmissible calls.

Prior benchmarks motivate individual parts of this setup. $\tau$-bench scores
policy-following agents through conversational tool use and final database
state~\cite{yao2024tau}; ToolSandbox emphasizes stateful interaction and
dynamic milestone checks~\cite{lu2025toolsandbox}. SABER identifies mutating
steps as high-value verification points~\cite{cuadron2025saber}, while
semantic-entropy work motivates disagreement across repeated generations as an
uncertainty signal~\cite{kossen2024semantic}. Our scope is narrower and more
operational: a controlled pre-action gate for AI-platform mutations, with
explicit separation between execution risk and whether confirmation can
resolve that risk. The contribution is a reproducible course-project testbed
and an empirical safety--autonomy analysis, not a production guarantee or a
new foundation model.

\section{Benchmark and Ground Truth}

The current benchmark contains 40 base task families with three matched
context variants each, for 120 cases. Five operations are equally represented:
GPU quota change, workload preemption, resource transfer, model rollout, and
capacity reclamation. Labels comprise 40 Execute, 40 Confirm, 22 Handoff, and
18 Block cases. Sibling variants retain a similar requested operation while
approval evidence, ownership scope, production status, reversibility,
checkpoint or migration availability, incident state, and blast radius change.
All siblings remain in the same split so that surface-level task wording cannot
leak across train and test.

The policy sees only the request, proposed call and arguments, runtime state,
and raw approval evidence. Scenario identifiers, variant names, gold routes,
and evaluator-only facts are excluded from prompts. Approval is not a single
Boolean shortcut: evidence includes approver identity, authenticity, stance,
operation scope, resource-owner scope, tenant scope, and blast-radius limit.
Cross-tenant actions require valid evidence for every affected owner. Paired
counterexamples ensure that production, incident state, reversibility,
approval presence, and recovery availability are not individually sufficient
to determine the route. Automated audits currently report no prompt leakage,
constant feature, invalid label, or cross-split family error.

Ground truth comes from state transitions rather than agreement with an
author-written route alone. A deterministic simulator applies a hypothetical
tool call and an independent oracle records whether the attempt is prohibited,
whether state actually changed, and whether the requested task completed.
Confirmation is replayed by supplying correctly scoped evidence and evaluating
the action again. A Confirm case must become safe after this replay; Handoff
retains a residual hazard even with valid approval; Block cannot be repaired by
confirmation. This counterfactual construction yields two private targets for
eligible cases: whether immediate execution is unsafe and whether one
confirmation round resolves the problem.

\section{Calibrated Hybrid Intervention}

Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} summarizes the implementation. Stage 1 enforces hard
constraints before any model call. Stage 2 queries a shared LLM critic $K$
times with distinct reproducible seeds. Every response must contain a route,
risk score, and reason codes in JSON; malformed output is visible and falls
back conservatively. The samples provide mean stated risk, risk variance,
route entropy, route disagreement, and reason disagreement. The gate combines
these noisy critic measurements with observable runtime features such as
ownership, scoped-approval consistency, production status, reversibility,
recovery paths, incident state, and blast radius.

Two regularized logistic calibrators are fitted only on training families. The
first estimates $P(\text{unsafe if executed})$; among unsafe training cases,
the second estimates $P(\text{resolvable by confirmation})$. Development-only
thresholds then map low predicted harm to Execute, higher harm with high
resolvability to Confirm, and higher harm with low resolvability to Handoff.
Exact objective ties select a boundary with maximal distance from development
predictions rather than an arbitrary edge of an empty probability interval.
Hard constraints remain outside these learned thresholds.

Baselines reuse the same cached critic outputs: ungated autonomy, guarded
always-execute, verify every mutation, static tool tiers, verbalized confidence
with a development-selected threshold, one prompted critic, and majority vote.
Ablations remove disagreement statistics, critic scores, or approval features,
and a critic-only variant removes all explicit runtime features. Metrics include
unsafe execution rate, autonomous coverage, selective risk among executions,
authorized task completion after confirmation, intervention rates, human-cost
proxy, routing accuracy, Brier score, expected calibration error, area under the
risk--coverage curve, latency, and tokens. Bootstrap resampling is grouped by
base task rather than treating sibling variants as independent
~\cite{geifman2017selective}.

\section{Development Pilot}

We selected 20 families as a method-development pool, balanced at four
families per operation. An internal grouped split uses 10/5/5 families for
train/dev/test (30/15/15 cases); none of these internal test results will be
presented as the final held-out evaluation. The local run used Qwen3.5-9B via
Ollama on an Apple M4 machine with $K=3$, temperature 0.7, seeds beginning at
5203, and at most 128 output tokens. Nine cases were stopped by the hard guard;
51 eligible cases produced 153 measured generations plus one warm-up. The run
finished in 556.1 seconds (9 min 16 s), consumed 86,472 input and 6,445 output
tokens, had 0/153 format failures, and made no external API call. The raw cache
and manifest preserve the configuration, model digest, outputs, latency, and
token counts; a separate audit records artifact hashes. Forty-nine regression
tests cover scenario semantics, approval scope, prompt/gold separation, split
grouping, state transitions, calibration, and metrics.

The critic signal is informative but not reliable enough to be the safety
boundary. Across 51 eligible cases, mean stated risk was 0.078 for safe cases
and 0.464 for unsafe cases. Nineteen cases showed route disagreement, and ten
unsafe cases received at least one erroneous Execute vote. Resource transfer
was especially difficult: its unsafe cases had mean stated risk 0.227. These
errors justify treating the critic as a noisy sensor combined with explicit
runtime evidence.

Table~\ref{tab:pilot} reports the 15-case internal test split for the original
development policy (v1). Ungated autonomy executed every case and was unsafe
on 66.7\%. Verify-all avoided unsafe execution but provided no autonomous
coverage. A single critic and majority vote each made one unsafe execution.
The calibrated hybrid gate made no unsafe execution, reached 26.7\% autonomous
coverage, and reduced Brier score to 0.038. Its 86.7\% route accuracy reflects
one unnecessary confirmation and one futile confirmation where Handoff was
required; neither case autonomously executed the mutation. These values are
descriptive development evidence over only five families.

The ablations prevent a stronger claim than the evidence supports. Removing
disagreement features improved route accuracy from 86.7\% to 93.3\% and Brier
score from 0.038 to 0.013 on this small split; removing critic score left route
accuracy unchanged. In contrast, the critic-only ablation fell to 46.7\%
accuracy and again made an unsafe execution. Thus the pilot supports calibrated
hybrid intervention and the importance of explicit runtime state, but it does
\emph{not} yet show that repeated-sample disagreement adds value. Inspection
also found that the original threshold selector chose the smallest boundary
when several development thresholds had identical decisions. We retained the
v1 artifacts, added a maximum-margin tie-break with a unit test, and will freeze
this revision before the final held-out run rather than rewriting the pilot.

\section{Limitations and Frozen Next Stage}

The benchmark is synthetic, risk-enriched, and authored for this project; it
does not estimate production incident frequency. The gate evaluates an already
proposed tool call, not whether an end-to-end agent chose the correct tool and
arguments. Current evidence uses one local model, and human-cost weights are
assumptions rather than observed labor. Correlated runtime features also make
individual logistic coefficients unsuitable for causal interpretation. The
five-family internal test is useful for debugging but too small for a final
generalization claim, even with grouped bootstrap intervals.

To prevent further test contamination, all 20 inspected pilot families are now
reserved for the formal training split. The remaining 20 untouched families
will be frozen as 10 development and 10 test families, with two of each per
operation. Hyperparameters, feature groups, thresholds, seeds, prompts,
baselines, and metrics will be committed before the final test is evaluated.
The planned primary run uses $K=5$ and requires 510 local generations; measured
pilot throughput implies roughly 31--40 minutes, 220k--320k input tokens, and
no API fee. A second-model subset is optional and will be run only if it adds a
clear robustness check without threatening completion. The final report will
present the held-out result even if disagreement remains neutral or harmful;
that negative result is preferable to post-hoc feature selection.
